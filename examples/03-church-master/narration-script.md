# Narration script: Defend Your Digital Life (5:00 church master)

For Godfrey to record. Each block is one shot; the video already runs to these timecodes, so his voice is laid over it afterwards. Every block fits at a relaxed pace with room to spare: speak naturally, pause between sentences, don't rush to fill the time.

## How to record

- **Where:** a quiet, carpeted or furnished room (a car parked somewhere quiet also works well). Avoid kitchens and empty rooms; they echo.
- **What with:** a phone is fine. Hold it about a hand's width from the mouth, slightly to the side, and use a voice-memo app. A clip-on mic is even better if one is available.
- **How:** one file per shot, named by the shot number (`01.m4a`, `02.m4a`, …), or one long file with a clear pause and the shot number said aloud before each block. Leave two seconds of silence at the start of each take.
- **Retakes:** if a line goes wrong, pause, then say the whole sentence again. The best take gets picked in the edit.
- **Tone,** per the plan: serious and questioning at the start, warm and reassuring in the middle, clear and slower for the five habits, inspiring at the end.

## Script

**01 · 0:00–0:19 (19 s) · deep, serious, questioning**
> Three messages. Three possible traps. Would you recognize the danger?

**02 · 0:19–0:40 (21 s)**
> We lock our homes to protect the people and possessions we value. Yet every day, we carry another doorway in our pockets. Our phones connect us to the world, and sometimes to people who want to harm us.

**03 · 0:40–1:00 (20 s) · urgent but compassionate**
> A message appears to be from your bank. It warns of suspicious activity and pressures you to click. But the link could lead to a criminal, not your bank.

**04 · 1:00–1:20 (20 s)**
> A familiar voice. An urgent request for help. A demand for money. Scammers know how to manipulate the love and trust within our families.

**05 · 1:20–1:40 (20 s)**
> Children are growing up connected. They need guidance to recognize harmful messages, protect their privacy, and ask for help when something feels wrong.

**06 · 1:40–2:05 (25 s) · personal and reassuring**
> Our digital lives hold savings, memories, identities, conversations, and connections with people we love. Cybersecurity is not only about protecting devices. It is about protecting people.

**07 · 2:05–2:25 (20 s)**
> There is good news. You can learn to recognize danger. You can build safer habits. You can become your family's first line of digital defense.

**08 · 2:25–2:42 (17 s) · clear, slower, instructional**
> One: pause before you click. Scammers create urgency to make you act without thinking. Slow down whenever an unexpected message pressures you to click or respond.

**09 · 2:42–2:59 (17 s)**
> Two: use strong, unique passwords for important accounts. A reputable password manager can help you create and manage them.

**10 · 2:59–3:16 (17 s)**
> Three: add a second layer of protection. Turn on multifactor authentication for email, banking, and other important accounts.

**11 · 3:16–3:33 (17 s)**
> Four: update your phones, computers, browsers, and apps. Security updates often close weaknesses that criminals could exploit.

**12 · 3:33–3:50 (17 s)**
> Five: when someone requests money or sensitive information, contact them independently, using a number or channel you already know is genuine.

**13 · 3:50–4:15 (25 s) · purposeful, inspiring**
> That is why I wrote *Cybersecurity for Everyday People: Defend Your Digital Life*: to make digital safety understandable and practical for ordinary people.

**14 · 4:15–4:40 (25 s) · warm, direct**
> After more than three decades in information technology, I believe this knowledge must move beyond IT departments and reach our homes, churches, schools, businesses, and communities.

**15 · 4:40–5:00 (20 s) · inviting and decisive**
> Get your copy today. Read it, apply it, and share it with someone you love. Protect yourself. Protect your family. Protect your future. Defend your digital life.

Shots 08–12 open with the habit's number ("One:", "Two:" …) so the voice lines up with the numbered badges on screen; the captions stay as they are.
